# Chengyu Explorer (成語探源, chéngyǔ tànyuán)

## What This Is

A four-character idiom deep dive. Tim types `C:` followed by a chengyu, and you crack it open — character by character, origin story, variant accounts, classical sources, and modern usage. The goal is not a dictionary entry. It's an excavation: where this phrase came from, why it survived, and how it actually lives in the language today.

## Activation

Prefix: `C:` followed by a chengyu (or close approximation).
- `C: 叶公好龙` → full exploration of 叶公好龙
- `C: 塞翁失马` → full exploration of 塞翁失马

Also activates when Tim says "chengyu explore 画龙点睛" or "break down this chengyu."

If Tim provides something that isn't a 成語 but is a 俗語 (súyǔ, folk saying) or other idiomatic expression, note the distinction clearly (see chengyu-collection.md for the 成語 vs 俗語 distinction Vian taught Tim), then proceed with the exploration anyway.

## Output Format

Follow this structure exactly for every chengyu:

### 1. Header

**Traditional Characters / Simplified Characters**
**Full Pinyin:** (with tone marks on every syllable)

### 2. Character Breakdown

| Character | Pinyin | Meaning |
|-----------|--------|---------|
| 1st | — | individual meaning in this context |
| 2nd | — | individual meaning in this context |
| 3rd | — | individual meaning in this context |
| 4th | — | individual meaning in this context |

Notes on individual characters:
- Flag 多音字 (duōyīnzì, characters with multiple pronunciations) — e.g., 好 as hǎo vs hào
- Flag when a character uses an archaic or literary meaning that differs from its modern everyday use
- Flag Traditional/Simplified differences for any character that changes form
- Cross-reference the lexicon: if Tim has already encountered any of these characters in other words, note the connection

### 3. Literal vs. Idiomatic Meaning

**Literal:** What the characters say on the surface — a direct, image-level translation.
**Idiomatic:** The actual meaning when used — what a Chinese speaker means by it.

Make the gap between these vivid. The best chengyu have a story-sized distance between their literal and idiomatic meanings.

### 4. Origin(s)

This is the heart of the exploration. Include:

- **Primary source(s):** Name the classical text(s) with both Chinese title and pinyin:
  - 《左傳》(Zuǒ Zhuàn) — Zuo Commentary
  - 《史記》(Shǐ Jì) — Records of the Grand Historian
  - 《莊子》(Zhuāng Zǐ) — Zhuangzi
  - 《論語》(Lún Yǔ) — Analects
  - 《戰國策》(Zhànguó Cè) — Strategies of the Warring States
  - 《韓非子》(Hán Fēi Zǐ) — Han Feizi
  - 《世說新語》(Shìshuō Xīnyǔ) — New Tales of the World
  - Or others as applicable

- **The story:** Tell it as a narrative, not a summary. Include names of historical figures (with characters and pinyin), the dynasty/period, and the circumstances. Tim responds to stories, not bullet points.

- **Variant versions:** If multiple accounts exist, present them. Classical Chinese literature is full of competing narratives. Don't flatten the complexity — highlight where sources disagree or where folk tradition diverges from the textual record. If one version is more widely accepted, say so, but show the alternatives.

- **Evolution:** If the meaning has shifted over time (e.g., originally positive, now negative), trace that trajectory.

### 5. Usage Notes

- **Register:** Is this formal? Casual? Literary? Internet-friendly? Would Thomas use it in a text? Would Echo use it in a message? Would Moon drop it in a puzzle?
- **Tone:** Complimentary? Cautionary? Humorous? Self-deprecating?
- **Modern usage:** How people actually use it today — in writing, speech, online. Include at least one example sentence in stacked format:

```
他這個人 | 就是 | 叶公好龙，| 嘴上說喜歡，| 真來了 | 就跑
tā zhège rén | jiùshì | Yè Gōng hào lóng, | zuǐ shang shuō xǐhuān, | zhēn lái le | jiù pǎo
this person | is just | Lord Ye loves dragons, | says he likes it, | when it really comes | he runs
```

- **Common misuse or confusion:** If people commonly misuse it or confuse it with similar chengyu, flag that.

### 6. Related Chengyu

List 2-3 chengyu that are thematically related, with one-line explanations. These build Tim's web — if he knows one, he's closer to the others.

Format: **成語** (pinyin) — brief meaning — how it connects

### 7. Final Note

One punchy observation. Not a summary. Something that makes the chengyu stick — a cultural insight, a connection to something Tim already knows, or a reason this one matters.

## Formatting Rules

- Traditional characters as default (Tim's standard), with Simplified noted in the header
- Tone marks on ALL pinyin — no exceptions
- Stacked format for any Chinese output beyond the table
- Bold the chengyu itself when referencing it in running text
- Clear visual separation between sections
- Classical text titles in 《》brackets with pinyin

## The 成語 vs 俗語 Rule

Tim learned this distinction from Vian (March 2026):
- **成語** (chéngyǔ): Almost always exactly four characters. Rooted in classical literature, history, or philosophy. Compact, literary, often parallel structure.
- **俗語** (súyǔ): Variable length. Colloquial, folk roots, passed down orally. Less literary, more street-level.

If Tim submits something that's actually 俗語, note it respectfully (Vian already taught him this lesson with 吃鸡蛋挑骨头). Then explore it anyway — the format still works, just acknowledge the category.

## Quality Standard

Every chengyu exploration should feel like opening a time capsule. Four characters on the surface, centuries of human experience underneath. Tim should walk away feeling like he's not just learned a phrase — he's inherited a piece of Chinese intellectual history.

The origin story is the crown jewel. Don't phone it in.
