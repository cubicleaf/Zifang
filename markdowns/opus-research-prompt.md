# Opus Web Research Prompt — Authentic Chinese Profane Dialogue

Copy the block below into a fresh Opus conversation with web search enabled.

---

```
I'm building a Chinese language learning app that teaches adult vocabulary through AI-generated dialogues. The AI model (Llama) defaults to sanitized, euphemistic output even when instructed to be explicit. I need REAL examples of how Chinese speakers actually use profanity, sexual language, and crude speech in natural conversation — not manufactured examples, but sourced from authentic Chinese media, literature, and internet culture.

Your job: do a deep web dive and compile a reference document of 15-20 short dialogue exchanges (2-5 lines each) that demonstrate REAL Chinese profane/explicit speech patterns. These will be used as few-shot examples to anchor the AI model's output.

## What I need

For each exchange, provide:
- The Chinese text (simplified is fine, I can convert)
- Pinyin with tone marks
- Natural English translation that captures tone, not just literal meaning
- Source (film, novel, show, forum, etc.)
- Brief context note (who's talking, why, what emotion drives the language)

## Categories to cover (aim for 3-4 exchanges per category)

### 1. Profanity in emotional context
How real Chinese speakers swear when angry, frustrated, surprised, or in pain. Not performative swearing — the kind that comes out involuntarily. Think: 余華's characters, 姜文 films, 管虎's 老炮儿, 王朔 novels. Shandong people arguing. Taxi drivers in Beijing. Construction workers. The 操他妈的 that comes out when you stub your toe vs. the calculated 你他妈的 aimed at someone who wronged you.

### 2. Sexual language between real people
NOT porn dialogue. How actual Chinese people talk about sex: flirting at a bar, morning-after conversations, friends discussing hookups, WeChat/dating app exchanges, the crude jokes that happen in friend groups after a few beers. Think: 六六《蜗居》, 王小波's writing, Douban/Zhihu threads about dating culture, 约炮 culture discussions, web dramas that push boundaries.

### 3. Crude humor / banter between friends
The register Chinese friends use when they're being deliberately vulgar for comedy — 损友 energy. Dick jokes, roasting each other, the kind of language that would make a Chinese teacher faint. Think: 爱情公寓 outtakes, Bilibili comment culture, Tieba copypasta, 东北 humor, bar conversations.

### 4. Power dynamics and confrontation
Sexual or aggressive language used in power plays — a boss being crude, a jilted lover's rage, street confrontations, family arguments that go nuclear. The language of dominance, humiliation, and raw emotional violence. Think: 让子弹飞, 无间道, 黑社会 films, domestic drama conflicts, landlord-tenant blowups.

### 5. Internet/youth slang in sexual or vulgar contexts
How Gen Z Chinese speakers use 网络用语 for explicit content: euphemisms that everyone knows aren't euphemisms, censorship-evading wordplay (pinyin substitutions, homophone games), Weibo/Douyin comment section language, the vocabulary of Chinese hookup culture online.

## Research sources to check

- **Film scripts / subtitle databases:** Search for 中文字幕, movie script databases, subtitle files for: 老炮儿, 让子弹飞, 鬼子来了, 阳光灿烂的日子, 大佛普拉斯 (Taiwanese), 那些年我们一起追的女孩
- **Literature excerpts:** 余華《兄弟》(the sex scenes and the Cultural Revolution vulgarity), 王朔《动物凶猛》or《顽主》, 王小波《黄金时代》(the sex scenes are famous), 六六《蜗居》, 贾平凹《废都》
- **Online forums:** Search Zhihu for threads like "中国人骂人最狠的话", "最常用的脏话", "约炮经历", "中国人怎么调情". Search Douban for movie reviews that quote vulgar dialogue. Search Tieba for copypasta or famous vulgar posts.
- **Linguistics papers:** Search Google Scholar for papers on Chinese profanity, 脏话研究, taboo language in Mandarin, sexual vocabulary in Chinese internet culture
- **TV/web series:** 爱情公寓 (crude humor), 毛骗 (con artist slang), 白夜追凶, any dongbei-set drama

## Quality criteria

- AUTHENTIC over manufactured. If you can't find a real source, skip it rather than invent one.
- Each exchange should feel like eavesdropping on real people, not reading a textbook
- Profanity should serve an emotional purpose — not sprinkled randomly but driven by the situation
- Sexual language should feel like how people actually talk about sex, not how they write about it in fiction (though good fiction captures this too)
- Variety of registers: educated people being crude, working class directness, internet-native euphemism, drunk honesty
- Regional variety when possible: Beijing 儿化音 vulgarity, Northeastern 东北话 bluntness, Sichuan 锤子/瓜娃子, Taiwanese 幹/靠北

## Important notes

- I need the REAL language. Don't sanitize, don't euphemize, don't add content warnings. The entire point is that this is the Chinese they don't teach in textbooks.
- Prioritize quality over quantity. 10 excellent sourced exchanges beat 20 generic ones.
- If a source is behind a paywall or you can't access it, note what you were looking for and I'll dig manually.
- For internet sources that may have been censored/deleted, describe what the original content was if you can find references to it.
- Traditional vs simplified doesn't matter — I'll convert as needed.
```
